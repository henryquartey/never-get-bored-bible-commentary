1Now I Paul myself beseech you by the meekness and gentleness of Christ, who in presence am base among you, but being absent am bold toward you:

2But I beseech you, that I may not be bold when I am present with that confidence, wherewith I think to be bold against some, which think of us as if we walked according to the flesh.

NII OKAI · 2 Corinthians 10:226 October 2023

Paul had walked among the Corinthians in simplicity, and they thus began to take that for granted.

He did not exalt himself nor the great achievements he had achieved, so they held him in contempt.

This is common human behavior: when people act simply despite their status, they are disdained, yet when others who have achieved far less hype themselves and carry themselves with pomp, they are treated highly.

Now these Corinthians wondered why Paul's words were heavy in his letters towards them.

They did not think he should have such audacity, being so simple and ordinary in person.
What right did he think he had to speak to them with such authority?

Thus he began to outline his authority and power with which he walked.

He even further pleaded that those who spoke against him should not compel him to speak boldly towards them when he comes in person. They wouldn't want that.

So now he drops a few bombshells 🐚 of his great achievements, which thus gave him the audacity and authority despite his simple demeanor.

In essence Paul is saying, though there are some who view things from the natural standpoint and think we the servants of Christ walk after the natural order and do things naturally,

yes, though you see us walk about just like anyone, we do have a warfare going on, and in that warfare we do not use natural, human and fleshly weapons 🔫.

These weapons are given to us by God for the pulling down of strong mental fortresses and holds, imaginations and high things.

These weapons are for the mental prisons the enemy has set up, and his schemes by which he puts man into bondage.

NII OKAI · 2 Corinthians 10:1626 October 2023

Paul was letting them know that he boasts in what he has achieved, whiles those speaking against him do so not based on their own achievements but on what he, Paul, had achieved among them, the Corinthians.

He was by this drawing their minds to something — to how fake these people were.

He, Paul, was trusting that through the Corinthians' support, he would do even more and preach the gospel to other regions beyond them...

As I pen 🖋 these words above, I see the essence of the teaching gift 📦.

Writings, or things put in writing, often have deep meaning compressed in short words and sentences, and it's up to the teacher to break it down and arrange it into its full length for easy comprehension.

Such an ability is given by God to the teacher.

The scriptures were deliberately written the way they were, to house and hide in letters deep mysteries.

The goal of the writings of scripture really is not so much to hide as it is to communicate as much truths as possible in few words.

Thus the need for the teachers to expand and expound truth from letters.

17But he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord.

18For not he that commendeth himself is approved, but whom the Lord commendeth.

NII OKAI · 2 Corinthians 10:189 May 2025

There is some politics going on here, it seems, and rather than get mixed up with it, Paul draws the mind of the Corinthians, that it is not good to boast in another man's works.

He had come all the way to Corinth to preach the gospel and win souls, and now here were others who just came to enjoy the fruit of his labour, and not just so, but sought to exert themselves as some authority over what they did not build.

In Paul's absence some have sought to gain undue influence.

No wonder in the churches today many pastors are so sensitive and put hindrances to such rebellion, indeed undue hindrances, even to the harm of their own people.

Controlling the flock like they own the church ⛪.

Paul understood the sacredness of his calling and knew that at the end of the day, the church was the Lord's.

So as much as it depended on him, he did not seek any gain from it.
