13Go and get thee in unto king David, and say unto him, Didst not thou, my lord, O king, swear unto thine handmaid, saying, Assuredly Solomon thy son shall reign after me, and he shall sit upon my throne? why then doth Adonijah reign?

14Behold, while thou yet talkest there with the king, I also will come in after thee, and confirm thy words.

NII OKAI · 1 Kings 1:1430 May 2022

Many thoughts regarding the politics of the day. But here are my considerations: firstly, God had promised Solomon would be king, and that by the mouth of the prophet Nathan himself.

So how come now Nathan has become a politician and trying to "help God"? Was he not supposed to leave everything to happen by itself?

And this is where I think we sometimes miss it as believers. We leave many things to chance. Of course, that is not to say that we are to run off pursuing our own agenda by whatever means possible, be it fair or foul means.

The point I see here is we are to do what is at all possible within our power to do with regards to the promises of God, and leave the divine part to God.

That also means God will not do what we must do as men.

As I muse over this verse, another thing also comes to mind. Remember when David slept with Bathsheba, God did nothing all through David's scheming and murder of Uriah, but rather sent Nathan after the event to call out David.

Why did God wait till that time? That is something worth pondering about.

Again, is it not interesting that Solomon, of this wicked union, was chosen by God to be king? Something worth thinking about.

NII OKAI · 1 Kings 1:4716 July 2024

That sealed it. Anyone can anoint another king 👑 and rejoice in it, as demonstrated by Adonijah,

But to sit on the throne of David the king, and have his servants bless him, and for him to acknowledge such, was more powerful a symbolism than any other.

Adonijah had all the king's sons backing him up, which in itself was a very powerful symbol.

The other sons, though stepchildren, had all come together to back up Adonijah, who was the eldest son at this point.

Amnon, the first and eldest son, was dead; the 2nd, no mention is made of his whereabouts; Absalom was dead in his revolt against his father; and now Adonijah was next in command.

And all the other sons of different mothers had converged to make Adonijah king.

This was a very powerful symbol.

How could Solomon, the youngest son, and of an opportunistic woman 👩 as Bathsheba, be made king 👑?

They can't let that boy rule over them all. They had hatched a plan and it worked so far.

And David their father knew his sons were right to want justice and the right thing to be done...

But David had sworn by the Lord to Bathsheba, and he would not renounce it.

He himself said that in

Psalm 15:4 ESV
in whose eyes a vile person is despised, but who honors those who fear the LORD; who swears to his own hurt and does not change;

That such an one would dwell in the hill of the Lord.

So David honored the word of the Lord God concerning Solomon, and honored his word to his wife Bathsheba.

Nathan was the one who rebuked David for his sins with Bathsheba, and one would have expected him to be the last to support her, seeing her selfishness.

But he was firstly a prophet and a man of God, and knew what the word of the Lord was.

And he chose the Lord's side, though that wasn't the fairest.

But God does not judge after appearances, but judges the heart, and also God does not count the sins of the fathers unto the sons.

The fathers must not eat sour grapes and the children's teeth set on edge.
