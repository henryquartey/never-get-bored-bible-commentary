1Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?

2The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed, saying,

3Let us break their bands asunder, and cast away their cords from us.

4He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh: the Lord shall have them in derision.

5Then shall he speak unto them in his wrath, and vex them in his sore displeasure.

6Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion.

7I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.

NII OKAI · Psalms 2:721 June 2024

This psalm is a messianic psalm, commonly regarded so.

But I'm trying to see if it was David musing about himself and his kingdom 👑.

Because he was indeed the Lord's anointed, his kingdom had been established by God,

He prospered wherever he went and dashed to pieces his enemies.

God could have said to him, "You are my son, this day have I begotten you", in the sense that he called David, but at a point in his walk with God, God considered him his begotten son.

Simply, this psalm is messianic indeed, though we can apply it to David, but it more easily applies to Christ, but with some extrapolation David can be roped in.

This is why it is more messianic than a musing about self.

8Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.

9Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.

10Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed, ye judges of the earth.

11Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

12Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.
