1And he entered into a ship, and passed over, and came into his own city.

2And, behold, they brought to him a man sick of the palsy, lying on a bed: and Jesus seeing their faith said unto the sick of the palsy; Son, be of good cheer; thy sins be forgiven thee.

3And, behold, certain of the scribes said within themselves, This man blasphemeth.

4And Jesus knowing their thoughts said, Wherefore think ye evil in your hearts?

5For whether is easier, to say, Thy sins be forgiven thee; or to say, Arise, and walk?

6But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins,( then saith he to the sick of the palsy,) Arise, take up thy bed, and go unto thine house.

7And he arose, and departed to his house.

8But when the multitudes saw it, they marvelled, and glorified God, which had given such power unto men.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:816 December 2021

Yes, to men, but Jesus was no ordinary man 👨. Was Jesus even a man?

9And as Jesus passed forth from thence, he saw a man, named Matthew, sitting at the receipt of custom: and he saith unto him, Follow me. And he arose, and followed him.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:916 December 2021

Jesus, the rabbi whose fame had spread far and near, now calls to this "sinful" tax collector. And immediately he heeds to the call 🤙.

Is this how it happened, or it took a while for Matthew to warm up into the role of a disciple?

I think it happened as stated. Jesus calls Matthew, and what a great privilege to follow such a man of God.

Matthew saw the blessedness of the calling and heeded. I can imagine Matthew looking at Jesus as he passed with such a glow in his eyes 👀; Matthew's eyes 👀 sparkled ✨ with admiration of this prophet whom some believed was the Messiah.

As he passed, Matthew watched. Ah, alas he can catch 🤼‍♂️ a glimpse close up of Jesus. That wonderful man from Nazareth.

Then to his amazement Jesus inches closer. This can't be happening 😊. He really is passing this close to my collector's booth.

Suddenly he stops and says 👄 "Follow me". What, did I hear right...? But then Jesus is not moving either, looking straight at Matthew with such love 😍, he nods his head.

Yes, follow me, his smile ☺ seems to say. So I heard right. Oh, this can't be happening, and like in the case of Zacchaeus: tonight I will be dining in your house 🏡

10And it came to pass, as Jesus sat at meat in the house, behold, many publicans and sinners came and sat down with him and his disciples.

11And when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto his disciples, Why eateth your Master with publicans and sinners?

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:1116 December 2021

What were Pharisees doing in Matthew's house 🏡? I understand the many tax collectors and sinners were Matthew's friends.

But the Pharisees, who invited them? And why did it seem they were everywhere Jesus was, including in people's homes?

It's likely this was a party 🎉 opened to all. And Jesus present in anyone's home naturally drew crowds.

And the Pharisees, I guess, invited themselves in.
They had become like the custodians of the traditions of the fathers.

And Jesus was just messing their theology up and winning 🥇 the common people over. They just hated it.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:1112 October 2022

Again, all of scripture is within a certain context.

This does not mean men of God should go about eating in the houses of sinners because Jesus did so.

It's all within a context, and the context must be understood in order not to misapply the holy scriptures.

This is very important.

12But when Jesus heard that, he said unto them, They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick.

13But go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice: for I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:1317 December 2021

What a punch 👊.

Go and learn paa. Sometimes, in fact many times, as we read the Bible we miss the import of what is being said because we just glance.

I mean, these are teachers of the law ⚖, scribes et al. And Jesus makes a statement that they should go and learn. What a 👊. They just hated Jesus some more after this, I believe.

Jesus actually took that verse from Hos 6:6... Now this raises another question 🙋 or observation — Jesus was well-versed in the scriptures. He read them and memorised his own words/scripture.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:1312 October 2022

This verse seems akin to the scripture that talks about the kind of fast God requires:

Isaiah 58:6 KJV
Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?

So also, sacrifices are important, but mercy really is what God requires.

14Then came to him the disciples of John, saying, Why do we and the Pharisees fast oft, but thy disciples fast not?

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:1417 December 2021

Wei nyinaa Y3 asem, as we would say in Ghana. Did they feel that if Jesus was indeed the Christ, he ought to be more spiritual?

Seems so. Jesus and his followers were too carnal. Yet in this carnality he got results that none other did get.

He 🧗‍♂️ graciously gave them a good answer.

15And Jesus said unto them, Can the children of the bridechamber mourn, as long as the bridegroom is with them? but the days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken from them, and then shall they fast.

16No man putteth a piece of new cloth unto an old garment, for that which is put in to fill it up taketh from the garment, and the rent is made worse.

17Neither do men put new wine into old bottles: else the bottles break, and the wine runneth out, and the bottles perish: but they put new wine into new bottles, and both are preserved.

18While he spake these things unto them, behold, there came a certain ruler, and worshipped him, saying, My daughter is even now dead: but come and lay thy hand upon her, and she shall live.

20And, behold, a woman, which was diseased with an issue of blood twelve years, came behind him, and touched the hem of his garment:

21For she said within herself, If I may but touch his garment, I shall be whole.

22But Jesus turned him about, and when he saw her, he said, Daughter, be of good comfort; thy faith hath made thee whole. And the woman was made whole from that hour.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:2212 October 2022

Matthew's account makes it sound like the woman intended to touch Jesus's garment, and just when she did, Jesus turns around and proclaims healing and wholeness to her.

But this is not the full story. Matthew sums it up beautifully, whiles the other accounts give us the details.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:2214 January 2023

Matthew's account gives the impression of the woman getting healed after Jesus had turned because of her touch and told her her faith had made her whole.

But other accounts fill in the gaps and bring the meaning out clearer.

That is, the woman got her own healing when she touched the hem of his garment.

So strong was the power that Jesus noticed virtue had gone out of him.

35And Jesus went about all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing every sickness and every disease among the people.

36But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.

NII OKAI · Matthew 9:3617 December 2021

Jesus was so unconventional, crowds did not move him. Fame and popularity he avoided, and when he healed the sick 🤮 he hid it.

I know in that light ☀ it is easy to draw comparisons with now, where we advertise miracles and draw crowds by them.

But let's not forget the times and dispensations. Jesus at this time was a Lamb, yet to be slain.

It was not time to reign as king, yet the people wanted to make him king 👑.

Not to mention how the very leaders sought to kill him.

He had to bide his time wisely. Not to attract attention or do anything before the time.

But after he rose up from the dead, he himself gave command to make disciples of the whole world 🗺, and the whole world we will disciple into truth.

37Then saith he unto his disciples, The harvest truly is plenteous, but the labourers are few;
